# Muscle Mystery full narration transcript

Prepared 5 November 2026. Original AI-assisted script for group review. Adam is fictional; the voice is intended to be generated with Fish Audio S2.1 Pro. All eight clips use one voice. The paragraphs below are the exact spoken wording in `narration.json`.

## Meet Adam

File: `intro.mp3` · 34 words

Hi, I'm Adam. I'm twelve, and I love soccer. I already know I have Duchenne muscular dystrophy, or DMD. I'm curious about what happens inside my muscles. Will you explore the clues with me?

Display caption: Adam is curious about the science inside his muscles.

## Energy for a muscle pull

File: `cell-energy.mp3` · 37 words

Muscles need usable energy to contract. Inside a muscle cell, mitochondria help release that energy from food. Watch what happens when energy is available. In this model, the muscle cell pulls and becomes shorter when it contracts.

Display caption: Mitochondria help release usable energy from food.

## A cell team

File: `cell-parts.mp3` · 34 words

Each cell part has a job. The nucleus holds DNA instructions. The membrane controls what enters and leaves. Cytoplasm is the space where many reactions happen. Together, these parts help a muscle cell work.

Display caption: Cell parts do different jobs and work together.

## Instructions for support

File: `gene.mp3` · 35 words

My cells use DNA instructions to make proteins. A change in the DMD gene means they make little or no working dystrophin. Dystrophin normally helps support the muscle cell from inside, close to its membrane.

Display caption: A change in the DMD gene affects dystrophin production.

## Make a fair comparison

File: `support-intro.mp3` · 36 words

Let's compare two muscle cells. Both get the same energy and the same number of contractions. We change only their dystrophin support. Predict which membrane will be more likely to stay protected as the cells pull.

Display caption: Keep energy and contractions the same. Compare dystrophin support.

## Why damage can build up

File: `support-result.mp3` · 38 words

Both cells can contract at first. Without dystrophin's internal support, membrane damage is more likely. Too much calcium can enter and injure mitochondria. Over time, damaged muscle fibres can be lost. Our quick model speeds up that process.

Display caption: Missing support makes membrane damage more likely over time.

## From cells to stairs

File: `tissue.mp3` · 38 words

Muscle cells form tissue. Tissues make a muscle organ, which works in the musculoskeletal system. Fat and scar tissue cannot make an active muscle pull. When working fibres are lost, muscles weaken. That can make climbing stairs harder.

Display caption: Loss of working muscle fibres can weaken a whole muscle.

## Keep asking questions

File: `closing.mp3` · 40 words

Now I understand how a change inside cells can affect movement. DMD isn't caused by a lack of effort. There isn't a cure yet, but care helps people live well, and research keeps moving forward. Thanks for investigating with me!

Display caption: DMD is not a lack of effort. Care and research matter.
